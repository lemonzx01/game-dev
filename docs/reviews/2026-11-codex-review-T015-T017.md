# Independent review of T015–T017

Codex reviewed local origin/main at f424dae47b20bfeb5b918d8765c950558a33f55b. The three handoffs, acceptance cases, action selection, navigation, economy, XP, checkpoint and canonical writers were compared against the task board and architecture. Verdict: changes requested. **12 findings: 2 High, 9 Medium, 1 Low.** Address the two trade-route findings together.

## High

1. **An unreachable buyer holds the town's trader slot indefinitely.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Paths.cs:73, Simulation/SimulationWorld.Settlements.cs:177, Simulation/SimulationWorld.Trade.cs:80. Loading debits home stock and reserves a slot before proving the route. A failed outbound search sets a work cooldown but leaves TradePartner, OnTrip and cargo intact. On retry the same partner wins unconditionally. With the default one slot, a surviving trader can block every subsequent deal. **Minimal fix:** on a terminal outbound failure, switch to returning the same cargo, release the reservation once on arrival or death, and suppress immediate rebooking of the same unreachable deal. **Test:** enclose the paying buyer behind mountains, leave seller and carrier together, then assert exact stock restoration, zero payment, zero outstanding traders and bounded retries. Open a route and verify trade resumes.

2. **Long trade routes bypass the node budget and disappear from its counter.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Paths.cs:48–68, Simulation/Systems/Navigation.cs:26. A long request permits terrain.Length BFS expansions; only the number of requests is capped. An almost fully connected 4096² map with an isolated buyer can consume roughly 16.8 million expansions in a single search. LastPathExpansions omits all of them. This puts a synchronous map-sized search on the simulation thread precisely when the profiler suggests spare budget. **Minimal fix:** enforce a deterministic node allotment for long routes and count all expansions. If searches resume across ticks, persist and hash their continuation or use a bounded deterministic routing alternative. **Test:** queue long and local requests against a distant inaccessible buyer. Assert the combined expansion cap each tick, eventual local service and identical continuation across a checkpoint.

## Medium

3. **Settlement edits can make producing plots and stores inaccessible.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Paths.cs:114–118, Simulation/SimulationWorld.Settlements.cs:160–163. SetTerrain protects resource nodes and living occupants but accepts flooding an empty farm or town centre. The crop timer keeps growing the flooded plot, which can keep Harvest ahead of every other job. A flooded centre also prevents delivery and reading. **Minimal fix:** reject edits incompatible with an existing plot or centre unless there is an explicit settlement removal/conversion operation. **Test:** flood an empty planted plot and an empty centre through God commands. Verify rejection, unchanged terrain and continued harvesting and delivery.

4. **Promotion can strand a worker's existing load.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Trade.cs:127–130, Simulation/SimulationWorld.Settlements.cs:173–177. A guard vacancy can promote a non-trading carrier before it starts delivery. Guards cannot plan work, including delivery, so already harvested goods stay on the guard until death. **Minimal fix:** defer promotion while CarryAmount is positive or a collection action is still in flight. **Test:** remove the current guard just after another member harvests, force that member's next think, and verify one deposit, no silent loss and eventual guard replacement.

5. **An accepted capacity can create resources through signed overflow.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Settlements.cs:231–233 and :254–256. CarryCapacity accepts int.MaxValue. At skill level 2 the bonus adds one; capacity wraps negative before Math.Min, subtracting a negative amount increases source stock and Load records negative cargo. **Minimal fix:** widen the addition and clamp to available stock before narrowing the result, or reject settings whose maximum skilled load cannot fit. **Test:** use int.MaxValue capacity, level 2 and five available units for both plot and node. Require a five-unit transfer, zero source remainder and nonnegative cargo.

6. **Scholarship bonuses can turn an earned XP award negative.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Skills.cs:55–58. ScholarBonusPercentPerLevel accepts int.MaxValue. At Scholarship level 1 even adding the base 100 overflows the int multiplier. The XP product and accumulated long total also lack a defined upper boundary. This can violate the loader's nonnegative-XP invariant. **Minimal fix:** validate supported tuning and calculate the scaled award and remaining XP capacity in sufficiently wide arithmetic before mutation. **Test:** exercise the largest accepted bonus and an XP total close to long.MaxValue; verify the documented cap or rejection, monotonic XP and a loadable checkpoint.

7. **Crop scheduler references are trusted until the first post-load tick.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Save.cs:264–268, Simulation/SimulationWorld.Settlements.cs:159–164. The loader checks work kind and timing, but never cross-checks a CropGrow subject/token against a restored town and plot. An internally consistent, checksummed invalid state can load and then index outside the list; duplicate timers can instead accelerate growth. **Minimal fix:** after towns load, validate crop timer targets, multiplicity and agreement with GrowScheduled. **Test:** use invalid-state fixtures with valid integrity metadata for a missing plot, duplicate timer and missing timer. Require SaveFormatException before publishing the world.

8. **Settlement, trade and reading checkpoint invariants are incomplete.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Save.cs:331–333 and :374–379; Simulation/SimulationWorld.Settlements.cs:337–340; Simulation/SimulationWorld.Skills.cs:136–145. Examples include positive cargo with an invalid kind (delivery treats anything except Food as Wood), active TradeTravel without a buyer, mismatched TradersOut, and an open reading session with an unknown book or future start tick. Checksums and equality to a supplied state hash verify representation, not these domain relationships. **Minimal fix:** validate reachable combinations after towns and members load, reconcile live counters and validate unique book keys, catalogue bounds and session timestamps. Read collection lengths as unsigned before narrowing. **Test:** independently reject each inconsistent fixture with valid integrity metadata; include a legitimate trader returning with unsold goods and a legitimate mid-read checkpoint as positive controls.

9. **The main trade test can pass without charging the seller or paying for sales.** Tests/Simulation.Tests/T016Tests.cs:84–97. It checks buyer food but never reconciles west food. Zip truncates to the shorter event list; removing payment events and both coin mutations lets the empty Zip and unchanged-coin checks pass. The cargo inequality also credits unrelated workers' deposits, losses and loads. **Minimal fix:** assert equal sale/payment counts, match events by tick and parties, and reconcile both towns and individual trade cargo by good. **Test:** freeze unrelated production and consumption, load eight units, reduce buyer funds in transit so only three sell, then require exactly three units and their price exchanged, five returned, and the slot released. Removing the seller debit or the payment must fail.

10. **A render read scans the entire population once per town, repeatedly.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Settlements.cs:355–369; Game/Presentation/TownsPanel.cs:25–26; Game/Presentation/MapRenderer.cs:150. One call costs O(towns × entities); the town panel alone calls it twice, and map drawing calls again. This is a presentation read, distinct from an AI population scan. **Minimal fix:** aggregate living rows by settlement in one pass and share the immutable result for the completed tick, invalidating it on load. **Test:** verify counts after promotion and death; instrument repeated same-tick reads at several town/population sizes to assert one linear aggregation.

11. **Each trade-plan evaluation can scan every settlement.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Trade.cs:45–61; Simulation/SimulationWorld.Mind.cs:190 and :249; Simulation/SimulationWorld.Skills.cs:86. For surplus stock with no eligible buyer, both goods can require a complete town scan. Workers repeat that query during scoring, reading eligibility and work selection; the think budget counts agents and does not bound the cost of each query. **Minimal fix:** share a deterministic deal lookup per seller and economic revision, retaining a current-state check at load. Bound or index the search if large town counts are supported. **Test:** many idle workers in one surplus town, many ineligible buyers, then one buyer gaining funds. Count evaluations and verify the newly eligible nearest buyer and ascending-ID tie break.

## Low

12. **Founding can place a plot beyond the documented Manhattan distance.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Settlements.cs:93–105; Tests/Simulation.Tests/T015Tests.cs:107. Candidate offsets span a 7×7 square. Sorting by Manhattan distance does not exclude a corner at distance six when the nearer tiles are ineligible. The existing assertion uses a mostly grass site. **Minimal fix:** skip offsets whose Manhattan distance exceeds three. **Test:** make the only eligible grass tile offset (3,3), then separately (2,1). Expect zero plots in the first world and one in the second.
