# Independent review of R1 — species bodies, sleep and recovery

**Verdict: changes requested. 17 findings, comprising 3 High, 12 Medium and 2 Low.** Reviewed local origin/main at 417c011b1d27f93e4a7fd9da542636327822e600. Fix the live/load recovery divergence, the dependency of waking on the event log, and the ability to go to sleep for an extra day first.

## High

1. **Switching from Sleep to Rest gives the live world and its save different healing rates.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Mind.cs:596–599, Simulation/SimulationWorld.Sleep.cs:69–80, Simulation/SimulationWorld.Save.cs:528. SetResting(true) stamps the *old* Sleep kind before TryStart assigns Rest. This can also happen when a tired person abandons a walk home for Rest. The live anchor heals at 400%; load derives 250% from the new kind. An old death booking can consequently disagree with the loaded anchor; RunDeath throws on that mismatch (Simulation/SimulationWorld.Entities.cs:100–103). **Probe:** invoking the real transition on a wounded sleeper and round-tripping produced Rest/400 and Rest/250 with equal immediate StateHash; HP one tick later was 300400 versus 300250. **Minimal fix:** close both elapsed segments with the outgoing state, assign the incoming kind/rest flag together, then derive rates and book death once. **Regression:** interrupt travel-to-sleep with fatigue-driven Rest. Check fatigue, HP, first lethal tick/cause and immediate/per-tick replay across the save. Also exercise Sleep to Flee and Idle.

2. **A real attack goes silent when the event log is full.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.cs:136–147, Simulation/SimulationWorld.Combat.cs:162–175. Hurt applies damage regardless of whether Emit accepts Attacked, but only an accepted append runs OnNoise. Earlier unrelated events, including WokeUp records emitted by the previous attack, can determine whether the next group reacts. **Probe:** against the same sleeping town, saturating the event counter returned sequence 0 and left the villager asleep; with capacity the same notification woke it. **Minimal fix:** trigger the simulation noise from a confirmed hit independently of whether its observer event fits. Define the order relative to lethal damage and emit wake records only for live recipients. **Regression:** two identical actual hits with free/saturated telemetry capacity must produce identical damage, awake IDs and next decisions despite different event streams.

3. **Arriving home at dawn can commit a villager to sleep until tomorrow.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Mind.cs:510–511, :533, :709; Simulation/SimulationWorld.Paths.cs:105; Simulation/SimulationWorld.Sleep.cs:104–128. The walk or queued search can outlast the window. Both successful arrival and failed search call BeginSleep without checking the window, current job or needs again. At the exclusive end, WakeTick deliberately picks tomorrow. With the default calendar the boundary probe produced 499 → 2899. The current-action shortcut can also retain a pending Sleep after a guard promotion. **Minimal fix:** recheck eligibility at the actual bed-down tick, including guard duty; finish an expired intent and replan. Only compute a wake time for an eligible sleeper. **Regression:** force successful and failed routes to finish one tick before, exactly at, and one tick after dawn. Include a promotion during travel, and assert the villager stays awake when the intent expires.

## Medium

4. **Fight noise bypasses the candidate budget.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Sleep.cs:138–149, Simulation/Queries/SpatialGrid.cs:55–62. Each hit walks every ID in overlapping buckets, even when everybody is awake. A store can contain the whole town on one tile. H hits and N occupants cost O(HN) visits in the same tick, plus per-sleeper lookups, scheduling and event work. Grid order is deterministic and WokeUp does not recurse into OnNoise; neither bounds the workload. **Minimal fix:** coalesce overlapping noise and use an indexed sleeper set or deterministic budgeted continuation with an explicit wake-latency contract. Preserve pending work across saves. **Regression:** crowd a store, attack repeatedly and instrument visits, duplicate wakes, p99 and replay at the smallest budget.

5. **Turning Body off still changes canonical anchors and adds required work.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Mind.cs:61–67, Simulation/SimulationWorld.Sleep.cs:74–80, Simulation/SimulationWorld.Entities.cs:87–92, :247–254. Every Rest toggle rewrites the vitals tick and enqueues another finite death booking even at 100% unchanged rates. The anchor and scheduler differ from pre-R1 beyond schema bytes, and obsolete jobs accumulate until due. **Minimal fix:** bypass the vitals re-anchor when Body is disabled; skip rebooking unchanged effective rates where possible. **Regression:** replay a pre-R1 Rest-heavy fixture with Body off and compare canonical gameplay fields, scheduler entries and events tick-by-tick after normalizing schema/config additions.

6. **A sleeping healer can cast without waking.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Mind.cs:417–428, Simulation/SimulationWorld.Combat.cs:299–321. The heal scan and cast precede the sleep commitment return. An eligible sleeping mage spends mana, gains fatigue and heals a townmate while keeping its sleep action and recovery rate. The R1 fixture deliberately disables healers. **Minimal fix:** while asleep only process valid waking stimuli and timers, or explicitly wake and transition before casting. **Regression:** put a trained sleeper beside a wounded ally; check mana, target HP, fatigue and spell events before and after a genuine wake.

7. **An unreachable bed stalls unrelated paths for the rest of the tick.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Paths.cs:38–60, :95–108. The failed Sleep request returns from ProcessPaths itself. One unreachable home per tick becomes an accidental throughput cap for all queued requests even with ample budget left. **Minimal fix:** finish handling that request and continue the queue under its existing budget. **Regression:** enqueue an unreachable sleeper followed by reachable drink/work paths, leave sufficient expansion budget, and assert both later requests are served that tick.

8. **Quantization can turn a tiny sleep window into a full day; large days can yield a past wake.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Sleep.cs:94–109, Simulation/Core/Calendar.cs:13. Distinct per-mille endpoints can floor to the same tick. For a 10-tick day and 101–109, the wrap predicate is then true for every tick. The modulo expression also adds two ints before assigning to long: on a legal two-billion-tick day, adding the day to a positive offset can overflow. **Minimal fix:** specify tick discretization and handle collapsed intervals explicitly; calculate the forward difference in long. **Regression:** enumerate every boundary of a 7- and 10-tick day, wrapped/nonwrapped and collapsed windows, then check strictly future wake times on a two-billion-tick day.

9. **Sleep commitment suppresses urgent needs even when food or water is available.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Mind.cs:423–429, :497–500, Simulation/SimulationWorld.Sleep.cs:112–128. A fed creature can enter sleep just below the drink threshold, cross it almost immediately and never consider drinking until dawn. Accepted high thirst/damage rates can kill it beside water during that commitment. **Minimal fix:** let urgent needs break sleep or schedule the earlier of the threshold crossing and window end. **Regression:** a sleeper beside reachable water must wake and drink before a known lethal tick. Pair with a fed sleeper that stays asleep until the boundary.

10. **Awake healing conflicts with roadmap acceptance.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Sleep.cs:69, Simulation/Core/Vitals.cs:92, Tests/Simulation.Tests/R1Tests.cs:150–166; docs/design/realism-roadmap.md:24–29. The roadmap and TASKS require fed *and resting*. Realism-on gives awake workers 100% regeneration and the test explicitly requires that result. A creature above the eat threshold but below maximum also regenerates. **Minimal fix:** reconcile the faster-healing handoff with the owner’s acceptance rule. To implement the roadmap, define the fed threshold and permit a real zero regen rate in realistic mode (zero currently means default). **Regression:** independently vary fed/hungry and Work/Rest/Sleep; verify exact HP on both sides of a threshold while retaining legacy behavior with Body off.

11. **Wounds do not increase the preference to rest over work.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Mind.cs:489–515, docs/design/realism-roadmap.md:24. In a safe town, Rest is admitted by fatigue alone and work does not depend on health. A badly wounded, fed, low-fatigue worker has no injury-driven Rest candidate. Changing passive regen alone would leave that worker unable to choose recovery. **Minimal fix:** add an injury-based Rest preference with a recovery exit threshold, preserving urgent needs and danger priorities. **Regression:** two otherwise identical workers with different HP, stock available and outstanding work; verify the wounded one rests, then resumes work after recovery.

12. **Load accepts unreachable sleep state and inconsistent death bookings.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Save.cs:488–511, :528, :446–448, Simulation/SimulationWorld.Sleep.cs:85–90, :107. A valid enum suffices for Sleep even if Body is off or the species has no window. A saved walk-to-sleep that later calls BeginSleep can dereference a missing window. Resting can also disagree with kind, and the saved death tick is trusted independently of the re-derived rates. **Minimal fix:** after the entire entity and scheduler are restored, validate action/rest/travel combinations and a matching current wake/death booking where required; allow legitimate stale queue items. **Regression:** checksummed fixtures for disabled Sleep, SleepPattern.None, an idle resting entity and a mismatched death booking must fail load cleanly. Valid in-flight paths, sleepers and stale tokens must round-trip.

13. **A positive metabolism can round down to permanent zero.** Simulation/Core/Vitals.cs:48–50, Simulation/Core/Species.cs:99–103. At a legal base rate of 1 and a dragon’s 30%, the scaler returns zero per tick forever. The need never reaches its threshold and its death booking treats it as disabled. **Minimal fix:** carry fractional progress using fixed-point anchor arithmetic, including in the inverse death-time calculation, or reject combinations below supported precision. **Regression:** base 1, species 30%, ten ticks and a mid-segment save/re-anchor; expect three units with fractional support, or a configuration error. Explicit base zero must remain valid.

14. **The save test masks lost derived rates.** Tests/Simulation.Tests/R1Tests.cs:33–41, :169–183, Simulation/SimulationWorld.Entities.cs:247–254. SaveAsleep runs with zero hunger, thirst and fatigue growth, at full health, comparing a single end-of-day hash that omits rate percentages. Both timelines can produce that hash even when their first post-load vital reads differ (finding 1). **Minimal fix:** use wounded, non-saturated subjects with non-unit species rates; inspect actual vitals at load and every transition. **Regression:** cover awake, Rest, walking home and asleep; check fatigue recovery at 1× versus 2× and 250% versus 400% healing, then death due tick/cause in a separate damaging scenario.

15. **Wild animals ignore the specified sleep location.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Sleep.cs:116–125, Simulation/SimulationWorld.Mind.cs:621–624, docs/design/realism-roadmap.md:21. An animal without a settlement starts sleeping on its current tile even when isolated in open ground beside reachable forest or its herd. The handoff states this simplification, while the roadmap still requires cover or herd proximity. **Minimal fix:** use a bounded local search for cover/herd with a deterministic fallback, or record owner approval for sleeping in place. **Regression:** isolated deer beside reachable forest, herd already present, and no reachable cover. Verify position, fallback and the search budget.

## Low

16. **Species/day-cycle coverage can pass without the required population or any hunting.** Tests/Simulation.Tests/R1Tests.cs:70–92, :112–128. Zip silently truncates missing spawns and All succeeds on an empty wolf list. The rates test measures 60 ticks; the cycle fixture has zero hunger and checks three pose samples, so it cannot demonstrate more hunts at night. **Minimal fix:** assert exact spawn counts/IDs, compare needs over a full unsaturated day, and count real hunt starts in matched day/night windows. **Regression:** a missing wolf spawn or disabled hunting must fail the appropriate acceptance even if every remaining animal sleeps on schedule.

17. **Waiting for a path home displays the asleep pose.** Game/Presentation/MapRenderer.cs:280–291, Simulation/SimulationWorld.Sleep.cs:60, Simulation/SimulationWorld.Mind.cs:598–599, :621–624. A stationary villager with a pending home path already publishes Sleep; the renderer maps that to the lying frame although the simulation has Resting=false. It can lie down before ever leaving its work tile. **Minimal fix:** expose an actual sleeping flag through the presentation read model, or distinguish the travel action. **Regression:** hold the home path in a queue; assert standing while waiting, walking during travel, and a living, untinted lying frame after arrival.

## Verification and scope

- Ran `dotnet test Tests/Simulation.Tests -c Release -m:1` on the isolated review branch. The first restore could not reach NuGet (NU1301). Repeated the same command with RestoreSources pointing at a temporary feed made from installed packages: **117/117 groups passed, 0 failed**, including all five R1 groups.
- Read-only probes used the compiled Release assembly and the existing R1 town fixture. The transition and event-counter probes above exercise individual production methods; they are not additional passing suite cases.
- All inspected production VitalsAnchor assignments flow through EntityState.Vitals. Spawn stamps before ScheduleDeath; valid load stamps after restoring the mind. The divergence in finding 1 originates in live transition ordering. FatigueAt closes the outgoing interval before toggling Resting. Sleep to Flee/Idle uses the outgoing sleep recovery correctly on the inspected paths.
- The normal wake step is required work. On-trip residents skip the walk home and retain cargo/trip state when starting sleep; juvenile guard promotion is explicitly excluded. These are source traces. No Godot runtime or new performance benchmark was run for this read-only review.
