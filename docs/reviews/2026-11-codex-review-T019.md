# Independent review of T019 — combat, magic, dragon, taming and effects

**Verdict: changes requested. 15 findings: 2 High, 11 Medium, 2 Low.** Reviewed local origin/main at 271706a779d0fd5b8cc8ff18347475e60a04eca2. The most urgent fixes are rechecking hunt eligibility at execution and rejecting impossible adventure state before a loaded world reaches the UI.

## High

1. **An existing hunt can hit an animal that has since become protected.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Ecology.cs:105–106, :114–146; Simulation/SimulationWorld.Combat.cs:67–72. Selection excludes domestic animals and all pets for a tamed hunter. Execution checks life and, on impact, shared town. A wolf can select a wild deer, the deer can bond with another town during pursuit, and the wolf can still bite it. A wild wolf tamed while hunting sheep can likewise finish that hunt. **Minimal fix:** share a hunt-specific eligibility predicate between selection and execution; finish an invalid hunt before moving, charging fatigue or awarding XP. Keep retaliation policy separate. **Regression:** change allegiance mid-hunt using two real towns; assert no subsequent pursuit, damage, fatigue or Combat XP against the protected target, and verify a wild alternative can still be hunted.

2. **Loading accepts impossible ownership and mana anchors that consumers trust.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Combat.cs:275–282; Simulation/SimulationWorld.Save.cs:331–333; Game/Presentation/InspectorPanel.cs:61. A positive TamedTo need not identify an existing settlement. The inspector indexes that ID directly. ManaTick may be in the future, yielding negative displayed mana; trust can exceed the species threshold, and a feeder ID can point outside the allocated timeline. The ordinary save checksum does not establish these relationships. **Minimal fix:** validate timestamps and species-specific bounds, then resolve the owner and feeder after towns and death records are restored. Permit a real, deceased feeder. Reject inconsistent state with SaveFormatException before replacing the current world. **Regression:** load internally checksummed fixtures with an absent town, future anchor, excessive trust and never-allocated feeder; pair them with a valid bond that outlives its feeder.

## Medium

3. **Two simultaneous feeders can overwrite one another's progress and spend both towns' food.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Taming.cs:46–47, :55–83. Two agents may select an unclaimed animal before the first feed. The second scheduled step checks that the animal is alive and untamed, but never rechecks the now-established feeder. It debits food and resets the first feeder's trust. **Minimal fix:** revalidate the current claim, animal eligibility and feeder eligibility immediately before debiting food or drawing RNG. **Regression:** send two towns' feeders toward one deer at zero trust; after the first succeeds, the stale second action must preserve food, trust, feeder and RNG state.

4. **An abandoned attempt can lock an untamed animal for the feeder's entire lifetime.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Taming.cs:47, :71–83, :94–99. Even a first refusal sets TamerId at zero trust. Other feeders are excluded while that person lives, even after promotion to guard or permanent departure. The handoff's durable bond applies after successful taming. **Minimal fix:** distinguish a short-lived, deterministically expiring attempt from personal trust and final ownership. **Regression:** first feed refused, original feeder stays alive but stops trying; another eligible feeder must eventually be able to start at its own trust baseline. A completed bond must survive the same transition.

5. **Heal skips the fatigue cost charged by Firebolt.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Combat.cs:231–236, :254–261; docs/handoffs/T019.md, Combat cost. A successful Heal spends mana and restores health but never calls AddFatigue, contrary to the documented cost of each spell. **Minimal fix:** charge fatigue once after target and mana validation. **Regression:** for one successful heal below the fatigue cap, assert exact deltas in fatigue, mana and health; repeat with no eligible target and assert all three unchanged.

6. **Damage multipliers can wrap before division.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Combat.cs:89–94, :132; Simulation/Core/Species.cs:84. Valid long attack power can overflow even at level zero when multiplied by 100. At long.MaxValue the first product wraps to -100, so a potentially lethal hit is reduced to one HP by the final floor. The ally percentage can overflow as an int too. **Minimal fix:** enforce safe configuration limits or calculate percentages using wide intermediates and an explicit final cap. **Regression:** maximum accepted melee and spell power, maximum bonuses, a wounded attacker and dragon resistance; compare damage with a wide-integer reference and assert monotonicity.

7. **Mana and trust can become negative under accepted configurations.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Combat.cs:34–38, :210–215; Simulation/SimulationWorld.Taming.cs:89; Simulation/Core/Species.cs:95. BaseMana plus a positive aptitude can exceed long.MaxValue; full mana plus regeneration can wrap before the cap. Two large permitted trust gains below a large species threshold can overflow before Math.Min. This can make a live world fail its own load validation. **Minimal fix:** reserve room for aptitude when validating BaseMana, and add only the remaining capacity for both regeneration and trust. **Regression:** near-limit configuration and partially filled bars, then two accepted feeds, elapsed regeneration and a save/load round-trip. Values must stay in range throughout.

8. **The town leash only constrains wandering.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Mind.cs:377–401; Simulation/SimulationWorld.Ecology.cs:146–174. Chase and flee choose neighbours without WithinLeash. A hungry town wolf can cross the boundary chasing wild prey; a deer can flee directly out. The test at Tests/Simulation.Tests/T019Tests.cs:191–194 only samples one final position. **Minimal fix:** apply the tamed movement rule to each autonomous movement path, or document any owner-approved exceptions and implement deterministic return. **Regression:** start at the boundary with prey outside, then separately with an inward threat; inspect every step and progress back toward home when starting outside.

9. **Repeated perception scans multiply work in crowded cells.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Mind.cs:158–173, :194–195, :240–245; Simulation/SimulationWorld.Combat.cs:101–109, :178–185, :246–253; Simulation/Queries/SpatialGrid.cs:46–53. A successful tame decision can search the same neighbourhood three times. Defence selection repeats its threat/friends query. Counting allies visits the entire bucket even after reaching the cap. Co-located agents make local buckets population-sized; the count-based think budget does not cap candidate visits, and hits also perform scans in required action steps. **Minimal fix:** pass selected targets through one decision, short-circuit threshold counts, and meter dense queries with deterministic continuation where nearest/best must be exact. **Regression:** count candidate visits for dense guards, healers and feeders; assert the budget and stable ID tie breaks, not just mean tick time in a sparse world.

10. **Firebolt suppression is lost across event-drain frames.** Game/Presentation/EffectsLayer.cs:69–88, :104–107; Game/Integration/SimulationBridge.cs:217–234. If SpellCast ends one frame's page budget and Attacked starts the next, bolts.Clear removes the correlation. An immediate melee impact and a later projectile impact are both drawn. The current effects test supplies both records in one Handle call. **Minimal fix:** retain bounded, one-use suppression for pending matching outcomes across drains; expire on consumption, gaps and world replacement. **Regression:** handle the two valid records in consecutive frames before landing, then assert exactly one impact at landing and normal rendering for an unrelated melee hit.

11. **Effects from an abandoned timeline survive load.** Game/Presentation/EffectsLayer.cs:59–63, :101–107; Game/Integration/SimulationBridge.cs:199–210. The bridge clears events and increments WorldGeneration. The effects layer retains active projectiles, which can land over the newly loaded map without an event in that timeline. **Minimal fix:** clear active effects and correlations on generation change before consuming the new frame. **Regression:** load during a bolt flight and heal animation; assert no old impact, and verify the next new-world cast still plays.

12. **The active cap bounds retained sprites but not work or allocations for rejected effects.** Game/Presentation/EffectsLayer.cs:54–56, :74–90. Each dropped effect still constructs an Fx, and a dropped bolt also does a caster detail lookup and flight calculation. This is allocation churn under fast-forward, not evidence of a permanent memory leak. **Minimal fix:** check remaining visual slots before allocating or resolving a caster, while retaining the bounded bookkeeping needed to suppress matching impacts. **Regression:** process a sustained event flood at capacity, assert peak active count, measure allocations and lookups for rejected effects, then verify complete expiry.

13. **Durable bond history accumulates in hot memory without the architectural cap.** Simulation/SimulationWorld.Taming.cs:102–103; Simulation/SimulationWorld.Life.cs:15, :146–147; Simulation/SimulationWorld.Entities.cs:208–223; ARCHITECTURE.md:116. Pagination limits the returned page; the backing list still retains every bond, birth and death. Every StateHash walks that history. T019 adds a permanent entry to this existing limitation for each successful taming. **Minimal fix:** use the shared indexed milestone archive and bounded page cache, with a deterministic archived-history digest. Preserve records before eviction. **Regression:** exceed 256 milestones, evict the live event ring, save/load, then page from old and recent cursors; verify exact records, no duplicates, equal hashes and bounded hot storage.

## Low

14. **The claimed mid-taming replay and retaliation assertions can pass without proving their preconditions.** Tests/Simulation.Tests/T019Tests.cs:129–135, :220–232. Saving at tick 20 after spawning a deer and a dragon does not assert a feed, partial trust or active fight. The retaliation check permits a later hit to justify an earlier dragon strike, and its negative check excludes some possible victims. **Minimal fix:** checkpoint an observed partial bond and an observed active fight; validate attacks against a sequence-ordered set of prior aggressors, distinguishing retaliation from hunting. **Regression:** compare subsequent feed rolls, mana, per-tick hash and paged AnimalTamed history across reload. Exercise two aggressors, a bystander, and target death; assert no same-tick counterattack cascade.

15. **The spell and presentation tests do not establish exact costs or that the dragon is drawn.** Tests/Simulation.Tests/T019Tests.cs:148–165; Game/Tests/EffectsAcceptance.cs:69–89. Firebolt checks the event's advertised price; the Heal inequality allows overcharging. Synthetic spell records use the dragon as both caster and target, and the visual acceptance checks its species and an active hit effect, so disabling the dragon draw branch can still pass. **Minimal fix:** observe balances immediately around each cast and assert an observable rendered dragon. **Regression:** exercise insufficient mana, out-of-range, dead and same-town offensive targets, with no debit or hit; capture a known dragon pose and verify a nontransparent dragon footprint, including its mirrored orientation.
